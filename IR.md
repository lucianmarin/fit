# IR, Stable Glucose, and FIT: A Practical Framework for Insulin Resistance, Prediabetes, Type 2 Diabetes, and Obesity

> **Medical disclaimer:** This document is educational information only, not medical advice. If you have insulin resistance, prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, or obesity — especially if you take glucose-lowering medication (insulin, sulfonylureas, meglitinides, SGLT2-inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists) — talk to your physician before changing meal timing or fasting duration. Extended fasts can cause hypoglycemia, hypotension, electrolyte shifts, and medication interactions. Monitor glucose, do not drive hypoglycemic, and seek care for symptoms.

## 1. Goal: Stable Glucose = Lower Insulin

For insulin-resistant, prediabetic, type 2 diabetic, and obese people, the core problem is not just high glucose — it is high insulin needed to force glucose down.

In FIT terms:

- **Refined foods = noise:** refined sugar, white flour, isolated starches and oils flood the blood too fast (Speed Toxicity), strip the fiber matrix (Matrix Toxicity), and deliver incomplete signal (Signal Toxicity). See `README.md` Part II, §4.
- **Whole FIT foods = stable signal:** Class A whole plants + 5% Class B accumulation layer deliver glucose slowly, inside fiber, with polyphenols, protein, fat, and fermentation acids acting as natural brakes.

The FIT diet helps stabilize blood glucose by:

1. **Eliminating refined noise (Class D = 0%):** no sugary drinks, white bread, sweets, ultra-processed snacks.
2. **Preserving the matrix:** intact fiber, cell walls, whole seeds, legumes, vegetables, whole grains. Fiber slows gastric emptying and carbohydrate absorption.
3. **Pairing for deceleration:** legumes + whole grains + vegetables + nuts/seeds + fermented foods + small amounts of fat/protein/B12 foods blunt postprandial peaks.
4. **Fermented + fiber synergy:** yogurt, kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, natto, tempeh, miso improve microbiome diversity and produce short-chain fatty acids that support gut barrier and metabolic flexibility. See `DIET.md`.
5. **High entropy, low glycemic load:** Rule of 30, hyper-complex recipes, bitter signals, color spectrum — more signal per bite, less glucose per bite. See `README.md` §12.
6. **Supporting autophagy tone:** spermidine-rich + fiber-fed microbiome supports cellular cleanup and mitochondrial turnover rather than constant storage mode. See `AUTOPHAGY.md` and `DIET.md` Philosophy.

Result: flatter glucose curves, lower post-meal insulin demand, less hunger rebound, less fat-storage signaling.

Diet alone, however, is often not enough once HOMA-IR is high. Timing matters as much as content. That is where the HOMA-IR-based fasting interval comes in.

## 2. HOMA-IR in 30 Seconds

HOMA-IR (Homeostatic Model Assessment for Insulin Resistance) estimates how much insulin your body needs to hold fasting glucose steady.

```
HOMA-IR = fasting insulin (µU/mL) × fasting glucose (mg/dL) / 405
HOMA-IR = fasting insulin (µU/mL) × fasting glucose (mmol/L) / 22.5
```

Fasting, morning, no acute illness. Repeat 2x for reliability.

Rough interpretation (lab cutoffs vary):

- `<1.0`: optimal insulin sensitive
- `1.0–1.9`: normal
- `2.0–2.9`: early insulin resistance
- `≥3.0`: significant insulin resistance
- `≥5.0`: severe insulin resistance, weight loss is typically stalled without extended fasting

HOMA-IR does not diagnose diabetes by itself. Use it as a trend marker alongside HbA1c, fasting glucose, waist, triglycerides/HDL, and clinical assessment.

## 3. The FIT-IR Rule: Scale the Fast to the Resistance

> **Rule: Fasting Interval (hours) = HOMA-IR × 4h × 2**

- `4h` = pragmatic estimate for one HOMA-IR point worth of blood glucose clearance back toward baseline after a FIT meal.
- `×2` = safety / completeness factor: insulin-resistant tissue clears glucose slowly and stays hyperinsulinemic long after glucose looks normal. The second ×4h block lets insulin fall, lipolysis resume, and liver glycogen deplete enough for fat mobilization.

Note on terminology: this is often loosely called “eating window” but what is meant is the **fasting window / inter-meal interval** — time from the end of one substantial meal to the start of the next. In this document `fasting interval` means no-calorie time (water, plain tea, black coffee, electrolytes if advised by your clinician).

### Examples

| HOMA-IR | Calculation | Fasting interval | Practical form |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1.0 | 1 × 4 × 2 | **~8h** | Normal overnight fast, e.g. 3 meals, no snacking |
| 1.5 | 1.5 × 4 × 2 | **~12h** | e.g. 19:00 → 07:00 overnight |
| 2.0 | 2 × 4 × 2 | **~16h** | 16:8 time-restricted eating |
| 2.5 | 2.5 × 4 × 2 | **~20h** | OMAD / 20:4 |
| 3.0 | 3 × 4 × 2 | **~24h** | OMAD to 24h alternate-day pattern |
| 4.0 | 4 × 4 × 2 | **~32h** | e.g. dinner → breakfast after next |
| 5.0 | 5 × 4 × 2 | **~40h** | Extended fast: e.g. Monday dinner → Wednesday breakfast |

For **HOMA-IR 5, the interval should be at least ~40h to actually lose weight.** Shorter windows (16:8, OMAD) still improve glucose stability, but in severe resistance they often maintain weight because insulin never falls long enough to unlock stored fat.

This is a starting heuristic, not a prescription. Round to a sustainable schedule, re-test HOMA-IR every 8–12 weeks, and shorten the interval as sensitivity improves.

## 4. You Can Still Eat All Calories and Nutrients — and Lose Weight

An extended interval does **not** mean chronic calorie starvation.

1. **Eat to satiety in the refeed:** a full FIT refeed with legumes, whole grains, vegetables, nuts/seeds, fermented foods, plus your 5% B12 layer (eggs, dairy, sardines, liver/poultry) can deliver full daily or two-day calories, protein, fiber, B12, iron, omega-3s, and spermidine/polyphenols. See `DIET.md` Pairing Ideas.
2. **Weight loss comes from low insulin time, not just fewer bites:** when insulin is low for many consecutive hours, hormone-sensitive lipase is disinhibited and stored triglyceride can be mobilized and oxidized. When insulin is high around the clock from frequent refined meals, the same calories stay locked in storage.
3. **Less rebound hunger:** stable-glucose FIT refeeds avoid the spike-crash-hunger cycle. People often spontaneously eat the same or slightly less over 48h despite a 40h fast, because the refeed is satiating.
4. **Cellular housekeeping, not panic:** spermidine-associated autophagy + fasting-induced AMPK/mTOR signaling shifts the cell from hoard-mode to recycle-mode. See `DIET.md` Philosophy: Obesity Paradox and `AUTOPHAGY.md` §2.

In practice: fast clean, feast complete. Do not combine extended fasting with a nutrient-poor refeed.

## 5. Fat Loss Improves Sensitivity — a Virtuous Cycle

Insulin sensitivity gets better with carrying less body fat, especially visceral and liver fat:

- Less ectopic fat → better hepatic insulin signaling → lower fasting glucose and insulin → lower HOMA-IR.
- Lower HOMA-IR → shorter fasting interval needed (per the formula).
- Shorter interval → easier adherence → further gradual fat loss.

Example trajectory:

```
HOMA-IR 5.0 → 40h interval → lose visceral fat → HOMA-IR 3.0 → 24h interval
→ further loss → HOMA-IR 2.0 → 16h maintenance
```

Track waist circumference, fasting glucose/insulin, HbA1c, and how you feel — not just scale weight. The goal is to graduate to shorter fasts.

## 6. Practical FIT-IR Implementation

### What to eat when you eat (FIT baseline)

- Base: legumes, lentils, peas, beans, oats, brown rice, barley, vegetables, crucifers, leafy greens, mushrooms, berries, nuts, chia/flax, wheat germ.
- Fermented daily: natto/tempeh/miso OR yogurt/kefir/sauerkraut/kimchi + aged cheese in small amounts.
- B12/protein anchor (5%): eggs, sardines, chicken liver/breast, yogurt/kefir, aged cheese.
- Zero: sugar drinks, sweets, white flour, ultra-processed snacks, refined seed-oil-heavy fried foods.
- Water-first, no-calorie drinks during fast.

### How to extend safely

1. **Master stable meals first** for 1–2 weeks before extending time.
2. **Step up:** 12h → 16h → 20–24h → 32–40h, only as tolerated and clinician-approved.
3. **Keep fasts clean:** no calories, no juice, no “small bites.” Plain water, mineral water, unsweetened tea/coffee if tolerated.
4. **Break the fast with FIT, not noise:** start with vegetables/legumes/fermented + protein/fat, then whole grains. Do not break a 32–40h fast with sugar/flour.
5. **Sleep, walk, light strength:** Zone 2 walking after refeeds flattens peaks; preserve muscle with 2–3x/week resistance; 7.5–9h sleep.
6. **Stop/consult for:** dizziness, palpitations, hypo symptoms (<70 mg/dL / <3.9 mmol/L with symptoms), vomiting, chest pain, gout flare, pregnancy/breastfeeding, history of eating disorder, advanced kidney/liver disease, or use of insulin/insulin secretagogues.

### Sample 40h cycle (HOMA-IR ~5, clinician-approved adult example)

- **Day 1, 18:00:** full FIT dinner to satiety (e.g. lentil-mushroom stew + brown rice + kimchi + kefir + small aged cheese).
- **Day 2:** fast (water/tea, walk, normal activity, sleep).
- **Day 3, ~10:00 (40h later):** break fast gently, then full FIT brunch/lunch. Repeat.

Frequency is individualized — e.g. 1–2x/week 40h with FIT maintenance eating between, or alternate-day patterns. Do not stack 40h fasts back-to-back without clinical supervision.

## 7. Evidence Caveat

The `HOMA-IR × 4h × 2` rule is a **pragmatic FIT heuristic**, not a validated clinical guideline. The biology behind it — longer low-insulin time needed with higher resistance for lipolysis and glycogen depletion — is well supported, and time-restricted and alternate-day fasting show benefit for weight and insulin sensitivity in trials, but individual glucose/insulin kinetics vary widely. Treat the table as a starting point for discussion with your clinician and adjust by measured glucose, HOMA-IR trend, and tolerability.
