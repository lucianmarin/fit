# IS: How Beans and Fatty Fish Promote Insulin Sensitivity — Science for Slower Aging and Longevity

> **Medical disclaimer:** This document is educational information only, not medical advice. If you have prediabetes, type 2 diabetes, obesity, cardiovascular, kidney or liver disease — especially if you take glucose-lowering, blood-pressure, lipid-lowering, or anticoagulant medication — talk to your physician before changing diet or supplements. Fish oil / high-dose omega-3 can interact with anticoagulants and affect LDL in some people. Monitor glucose, lipids, and medication needs with your clinician.

Insulin sensitivity is how little insulin your body needs to keep glucose stable.

- **High sensitivity (low HOMA-IR, high clamp glucose disposal):** small insulin pulse clears a meal, insulin falls fast, fat mobilization resumes.
- **Low sensitivity / insulin resistance (high HOMA-IR):** pancreas must hypersecrete insulin, fasting insulin stays high, lipolysis stays locked, liver makes extra glucose, fat accumulates in liver, muscle, and visceral depots.

In FIT terms (see `README.md`, `IR.md`): sensitivity = high Signal / low Noise. Beans and low-trophic fatty fish are two of the highest-signal, lowest-noise levers for restoring it.

This document gathers the mechanisms and human evidence for **beans (pulses: beans, lentils, chickpeas, peas, soy)** and **fatty fish (sardines, mackerel, salmon, herring, anchovies + their EPA/DHA oils)** as insulin sensitizers.

## 1. Why Insulin Sensitivity Is a Marker of Aging Speed and Longevity

Insulin sensitivity declines on average with age, but it is not obligatory — and preserving it predicts survival.

1. **Centenarians preserve youthful sensitivity.** In euglycemic clamp studies (gold standard), healthy centenarians >100 y had whole-body glucose disposal ~34 μmol/kg fat-free mass/min — identical to adults <50 y, and ~45% higher than aged 75+ subjects (~23 μmol/kg/min). Their 2-h OGTT glucose was also lower than 75+ controls. They also had lower LDL, higher HDL, and preserved antilipolytic insulin action (normal FFA suppression).
   - Paolisso et al., Am J Physiol 1996; Paolisso et al., J Am Geriatr Soc 1997; Fink, Diabetes Metab Res Rev 2001 review.

2. **HOMA-IR predicts mortality in non-diabetics.** In NHANES III (n=5,511, up to 12 y follow-up), each higher HOMA-IR quartile increased all-cause mortality (adjusted HR 1.16 per quartile; HR 1.64 top vs bottom, HOMA-IR >2.8 vs lowest), especially cardiovascular mortality, after adjusting for age, BMI, CRP, lipids, BP, lifestyle. Effect was clearest in normal-BMI persons.
   - Ausk et al., Diabetes Care 2010.

3. **Familial longevity = better glucose handling.** In the Leiden Longevity Study, middle-aged offspring of nonagenarian siblings had lower non-fasted glucose than their partners at same BMI, IGF-1, and insulin — i.e. higher insulin sensitivity. The hallmark of all long-lived GH/IGF-1 mutant mice is enhanced insulin sensitivity.
   - Wijsman et al., Aging 2009; Johnson et al. review of IIS and longevity.

4. **Insulin resistance drives the diseases that limit lifespan.** Age-related insulin resistance is mechanistically linked to atherosclerotic CVD, dementia, frailty, sarcopenia, NAFLD/MASLD, cancer, and disability. Hyperinsulinemia itself is an independent risk factor for ischemic heart disease (Paris Prospective, Quebec studies).
   - Petersen et al. / EGIR; Wilcox 2005 review "Insulin resistance: a risk marker for disease and disability"; Barzilai-type centenarian literature.

5. **But nuance in very old age:** In the Toledo Study of Healthy Aging (n=991 non-diabetic older adults), higher log HOMA-IR predicted *lower* 5-year mortality yet *higher* incident frailty/functional decline. Interpretation: in midlife to early old age, low HOMA-IR = slower aging; in advanced frail age, some reserve may be protective. The target for longevity is to *stay sensitive as long as possible* so you never need compensatory hyperinsulinemia.
   - Toledo Study, J Gerontol 2021.

**Bottom line:** Flatter glucose, lower fasting insulin, HOMA-IR <1.0-1.5, high clamp disposal, low TG/HDL — this cluster tracks with centenarian phenotype and lower mortality. Diet that lowers insulin demand without malnutrition is a longevity lever.

Related FIT rule: `IR.md` §3 scales fasting interval to HOMA-IR. Beans + fatty fish lower the numerator — less resistance to clear.

## 2. Beans: 9 Ways Pulses Restore Insulin Sensitivity

Beans are intact plant cells: starch encapsulated in fiber cell walls (RS1) + viscous soluble fiber + resistant starch + protein + magnesium/potassium/polyphenols + alpha-amylase inhibitors. All act as natural brakes on Speed, Matrix, and Signal Toxicity (FIT `README.md` §4).

### 2.1 Intact cell wall = slow starch bioaccessibility

Unlike white flour where gelatinized starch is naked, bean starch sits inside intact cotyledon cells. Cell-wall fiber physically blocks alpha-amylase. Result: lower rate constant (k) and endpoint (C∞) of amylolysis, ~40% lower iAUC120 when 30-60% wheat flour is replaced by intact-cell chickpea powder, with no loss of palatability.

High amylose:amylopectin ratio and amylose-lipid RS5 complexes further slow digestion. Cooling cooked beans/potatoes creates retrograded RS3.

- Edwards et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2021 (PulseON cellular powder RCT); Reuter 2026 review of 52 pulse trials 2004-2025.

FIT mapping: this is Matrix preservation + Speed Toxicity reduction.

### 2.2 Low glycemic index + low glycemic load per bite

Pulses are archetypal low-GI foods (GI ~25-45 vs white bread ~75-90) despite 50-60% carbohydrate, because fiber + protein + fat slow gastric emptying and intestinal glucose transport. Smaller postprandial glucose peak → smaller first-phase insulin peak → less reactive hypoglycemia and hunger rebound.

Meta-analysis of 41 RCTs: pulses as part of low-GI diet improve glycemic control (fasting glucose, HbA1c, HOMA). Effect is strongest in insulin-resistant/diabetic participants and with higher dose.

- Sievenpiper et al. systematic review/meta-analysis 41 RCTs; Zhang et al. pulse dose studies.

### 2.3 Resistant starch → SCFA → GLP-1/PYY, liver, and muscle signaling

RS1-RS3 escapes small-intestinal digestion and is fermented in colon to short-chain fatty acids: acetate, propionate, butyrate.

- **Butyrate:** colonocyte fuel, tight-junction support, HDAC inhibition, AMPK/PGC-1α engagement in muscle (mitochondrial density).
- **Propionate:** hepatic gluconeogenesis substrate that paradoxically *lowers* hepatic glucose output via gut-brain FFAR3 signaling; inhibits cholesterol synthesis.
- **Acetate:** systemic FFAR2 signaling, appetite regulation.
- **All SCFA:** stimulate L-cell GLP-1 and PYY → slower gastric motility, lower intestinal glucose transport, higher satiety, better postprandial insulin economy.

2025 meta-analysis (23 RCTs, 19 in quantitative synthesis): RS intake lowered fasting insulin MD -2.39 μU/mL, HOMA-IR MD -0.58, HbA1c -0.14%, total-C -0.20 mmol/L, LDL-C -0.11 mmol/L, with no significant change in fasting glucose — i.e. same glucose with less insulin = higher sensitivity. Dose ≤30 g/d, >8 weeks, supplement delivery showed clearest effect.

- Front Nutr 2025 RS meta-analysis; Canfora/Wong reviews on SCFA-GLP-1-AMPK.

### 2.4 Second-meal effect

Beans at lunch lower glucose/insulin at dinner and next breakfast, even with identical second meal. Mediated by overnight colonic fermentation (SCFA still being produced 6-10 h later), lower FFA rebound, and improved hepatic insulin action the next morning. This is unique to fermentable low-GI foods; white rice/wheat do not show it.

Practical consequence: daily beans create a 24-h sensitivity halo, not just a single-meal effect.

### 2.5 Microbiome remodeling: fiber-degraders up, pro-diabetic metabolites down

16-week Singapore RCT (n=127 prediabetes, legume-enriched ~200 g/d mixed beans/chickpeas + low-GI rice + vegetable oil + herbs vs isocaloric chicken + medium-high GI rice + corn oil): both groups lost BMI, but legume group had greater HbA1c, total-C, LDL-C reductions, with increases in fiber-degrading species, glycoside hydrolases for pectin/arabinoxylan/xyloglucan, and shifts in bile acids and amino acids.

Related white-bean extract RCT (4 mo, 1.5 g WCBE before meals): alpha-amylase inhibition delivered more starch to colon, remodeled microbiota, increased SCFA producers, lowered glucose and diabetic complications.

Mechanistic threads:
- Lower circulating BCAAs → less mTORC1 overactivation in muscle/liver.
- Lower imidazole propionate (histidine-derived microbial metabolite that impairs insulin signaling via mTORC1 and blunts metformin) with high-fiber legume diets.
- Higher indolepropionic acid (IPA, tryptophan-derived) → better gut barrier via PXR, lower T2D/CAD risk.
- Altered bile-acid pool → FXR/TGR5 signaling → improved hepatic insulin sensitivity.

- Nature Commun 2025 legume RCT NCT04745702; Front Nutr WCBE trial 2022; Koh et al. on IMP/mTORC1; de Mello et al. on IPA.

### 2.6 Magnesium, potassium, polyphenols, and phytochemical co-factors

1 cup cooked beans ≈ 60-120 mg magnesium, 500-700 mg potassium, plus flavonoids, anthocyanins (black/red kidney), saponins, and phenolic acids.

- **Magnesium** is cofactor for insulin-receptor tyrosine kinase, PI3K/Akt, and glucose-transporter translocation. Low magnesium predicts HOMA-IR and T2D; repletion improves clamp disposal.
- **Potassium** supports beta-cell membrane potential and vascular function.
- **Polyphenols** inhibit alpha-glucosidase/amylase modestly, scavenge ROS, inhibit NF-κB, improve endothelial NO — reducing the oxidative/inflammatory noise that blocks IRS-1 signaling.

This is FIT Synergistic Cryptography: fiber needs minerals/polyphenols to unlock full signal.

### 2.7 Protein + fiber satiety, low energy density, ectopic-fat loss

Beans: ~8-9 g protein + 6-8 g fiber per 100 g cooked, energy density ~1.2-1.4 kcal/g vs cheese/meat ~3-4 kcal/g, white bread ~2.6 kcal/g.

Pennington 8-week controlled feeding RCT (n=36, HOMA-IR >2, 50-55% carb, 30-35% fat): Bean diet (4 oz pulses/d + meat/fish + bread/rice/pasta) lowered insulin AUC -2136 mg/dL·min (p=0.03) and HOMA-IR -1.4 (p=0.02), with -4.1% weight loss. Low-energy-density potato diet was equivalent for weight/HOMA-IR, proving matrix + energy density matter more than carb % alone.

Less visceral and liver fat → less diacylglycerol (DAG) → less PKCε activation → restored hepatic insulin suppression of gluconeogenesis; less ceramide → restored muscle Akt signaling. See also `IR.md` §5 virtuous cycle: fat loss → lower HOMA-IR → shorter fast needed.

### 2.8 Lipid and vascular co-benefits that feed back on sensitivity

Legume enrichment consistently lowers total-C and LDL-C (Singapore RCT q<0.02), lowers TG in second half of 6-week hypocaloric legume trial vs animal-protein control (p=0.021), and lowers systolic BP (-8% at 6 weeks, p=0.009). Lower TG/HDL and better endothelial function reduce lipotoxic and vascular contributions to resistance.

### 2.9 Human evidence summary for beans

| Design | Dose / duration | Insulin-sensitivity result |
|---|---|---|
| 41-RCT meta-analysis, low-GI pulse diets | ~100-150 g cooked/d median | Improved fasting glucose, HbA1c, HOMA; stronger in T2D/IR |
| Singapore 16-wk RCT, prediabetes n=127 | 200 g legumes/d (2 meals) | Greater HbA1c, TC, LDL-C fall vs isocaloric control; microbiome mediation |
| Pennington 8-wk feeding RCT, HOMA-IR>2 n=36 | 113 g pulses/d (4 oz) | Insulin AUC ↓, HOMA-IR -1.4, weight -4.1% |
| Iranian 6-wk hypocaloric RCT, obese women n=42 | 1 cup cooked non-soy legumes/d replacing meat | HOMA-IR returned to baseline after transient rise; TG ↓12%, SBP ↓8%, ALT -46%, AST -30% |
| WCBE 4-mo double-blind RCT n=90 | 1.5 g white-bean α-amylase inhibitor pre-meal | Lower glucose, remodeling to SCFA producers |
| RS meta-analysis 23 RCTs | RS ≤30 g/d, >8 wk | FINS -2.39, HOMA-IR -0.58, HbA1c -0.14% |

No single trial proves longevity; pattern across glycemia, insulinemia, lipids, liver enzymes, and microbiome is convergent.

## 3. Fatty Fish: 8 Ways EPA/DHA Restore Insulin Sensitivity

Fatty low-trophic fish — sardines, anchovies, herring, mackerel, wild salmon — deliver EPA + DHA preformed (bypassing inefficient ALA conversion <5%), plus high-quality protein, vitamin D, selenium, iodine, and (in whole fish) phospholipid-bound omega-3 with higher bioavailability than ethyl esters. In FIT classification: Class B accumulation layer, capped at ~5% of calories, low trophic distance, low bioaccumulation vs predatory fish (see `README.md` §11).

Insulin resistance is an inflammatory membrane disease as much as a glucose disease. EPA/DHA act on membranes, eicosanoids, and gene programs.

### 3.1 Membrane remodeling: fluidity, rafts, GLUT4

EPA/DHA incorporate into skeletal-muscle, liver, and adipose phospholipids within weeks, increasing membrane fluidity, disrupting saturated-fat-rich lipid rafts that sustain TLR4 signaling, and facilitating insulin-receptor mobility, IRS-1 tyrosine phosphorylation, PI3K/Akt activation, and GLUT4 translocation to the sarcolemma.

Omega-6/omega-3 and AA/EPA ratios fall; TG/HDL ratio — a cheap IR proxy — falls in parallel.

### 3.2 GPR120 + PPARγ → NF-κB and NLRP3 brake

- **FFAR4/GPR120:** EPA/DHA are ligands for macrophage/adipocyte GPR120 → β-arrestin-2 recruitment → TAB1/TAK1 inhibition → NF-κB suppression → less TNF-α, IL-6, IL-1β.
- **PPARα/γ:** EPA/DHA activate PPARs → adipocyte differentiation toward small insulin-sensitive adipocytes, increased adiponectin, increased fatty-acid oxidation via PGC-1α.
- Human data: 4.8 g/d EPA+DHA (3.2 g EPA + 1.6 g DHA) for 3 mo in women with obesity lowered fasting insulin, HOMA-IR (below 2.5 cutoff), TG, AA/EPA, TNF-α, and CD4+ T-cell TBX21/IFNG/GATA3/IL-4/FOXP3/IL-10/IL-6/TNF-α transcripts. Insulin/HOMA-IR stayed low 1 mo after stopping; TG rebounded — i.e. anti-inflammatory imprint outlasts lipid effect.
- T2D fish-oil trial 4 g/d (1.8 g EPA + 1.2 g DHA, 12 wk): RvE1 ↑3.1×, PD1 ↑2.7×, TNF-α -38%, CRP -45%, with in-vitro RvE1 suppression of NF-κB p65 and NLRP3.

- Front Nutr 2023 high-dose omega-3 obesity study; Zhang et al. 2025 oxylipin/SPM trial.

### 3.3 Specialized pro-resolving mediators (SPMs): resolution, not just suppression

EPA → E-series resolvins (RvE1, RvE3); DHA → D-series resolvins (RvD1, RvD2), protectins (PD1/PDX), maresins (MaR1). SPMs actively terminate inflammation without immunosuppression, enhance macrophage efferocytosis, upregulate FGF21, improve hepatic lipid oxidation and glucose tolerance, and enhance PI3K/Akt-dependent glucose uptake in muscle/adipose.

Obesity/IR states have low SPM tone; restoring EPA/DHA substrate restores RvE1/PD1. This is now proposed as the mechanistic bridge for inconsistent older omega-3 trials that measured only CRP/IL-6 and not SPMs.

### 3.4 Lowering lipotoxic intermediates: DAGs and ceramides

Chronic saturated-fat + hyperinsulinemia overload produces DAGs (activate PKCε/θ → serine-phosphorylate IRS-1 → block signaling) and ceramides (activate PP2A/PKCζ → block Akt). EPA/DHA:

- Lower fasting TG (-15-30% at 2-4 g/d), lower liver fat (MASLD trials), lower postprandial chylomicron persistence.
- Shift hepatic partitioning from esterification/storage toward oxidation (PPARα, FGF21).
- Lower ceramide synthesis substrate (palmitate) when fish replaces red/processed meat.

Result: liver regains insulin suppression of glucose output (lower fasting glucose/insulin), muscle regains glucose disposal.

Calanus/marine-oil 12-wk RCT (n=266 obese IR): 2 g/d calanus oil + lifestyle lowered HOMA; 4 g/d lowered HOMA -0.76 in mild IR (2.5-5.0). DHA-enriched fish oil lowered HOMA only when baseline >2.5 (Abbott et al. 2020) — i.e. corrects resistance when present, does not cause hypoglycemia when sensitive.

### 3.5 Adiponectin, irisin, sirtuin-1, PGC-1α axis

- **Adiponectin:** rises or is preserved with fish diets vs falling with high-saturated-fat diets; adiponectin → AMPK → fatty-acid oxidation + insulin sensitization.
- **Irisin:** omega-3 + glimepiride 12-wk RCT (n=70 T2D): FBG, HbA1c, TC, TG, HOMA-IR all lower vs glimepiride alone (HOMA-IR p=0.021), HDL higher, irisin higher. Mechanism: EPA/DHA induce PGC-1α → FNDC5 cleavage → irisin → white-adipose browning via UCP1 → higher energy expenditure.
- **Sirtuin-1:** higher in omega-3 arm in same trial; SIRT1 deacetylates PGC-1α, NF-κB, FOXO → mitochondrial biogenesis + anti-inflammatory tone. Converges with spermidine/SIRT1/PGC-1α pathway in `DIET.md` and `AUTOPHAGY.md`.

### 3.6 Triglycerides, liver fat, and vascular insulin delivery

Hypertriglyceridemia impairs muscle glucose delivery via endothelial dysfunction and competes via Randle cycle (FFA vs glucose oxidation). Fish oil dose-dependently lowers TG, TG/HDL, UA/HDL (UHR), MASP1 (novel T2D-risk lectin-pathway marker) — 12-wk dose-ranging RCT (0.31 / 0.62 / 1.24 g EPA+DHA/d, n=201 completers): FINS and HOMA-IR fell dose-dependently (p<0.01), FBG trended down.

EPA-rich fish oil in burn-stress hypermetabolism (1.5 g EPA + 0.6 g DHA/d, 3 wk, n=24): FBS, insulin, HOMA-IR, CRP, ESR all lower vs corn-oil control, Cohen's d 2.7 for FBS, 1.4 for insulin — proof of insulin sensitization even under extreme inflammatory stress.

### 3.7 Protein, vitamin D, selenium complement

100 g sardines ≈ 20-25 g complete protein (leucine ~1.5 g), ~250-500 IU vitamin D, selenium, B12, calcium (with bones). Protein preserves fat-free mass during fat loss (critical because muscle is main glucose sink); vitamin D status associates with lower HOMA-IR in observational cohorts (VITAL nested case-control context). This is why whole fish outperforms isolated capsules in dietary patterns: satiety + micronutrient matrix + replacement of saturated-fat meat.

FIT rule: prefer whole small fish over refined oil capsules; use capsules only as bridge if fish access is limited.

### 3.8 Human evidence summary for fatty fish / EPA+DHA

| Design | Dose / duration | Insulin-sensitivity result |
|---|---|---|
| 12-wk dose-ranging fish-oil RCT, healthy 40+ n=201 | 0.31 / 0.62 / 1.24 g EPA+DHA/d | Dose-dependent ↑EPA/DHA, ↓FINS, ↓HOMA-IR (p<0.01) |
| 12-wk omega-3+glimepiride RCT, T2D n=70 | 1 g fish oil (130 mg EPA + 90 mg DHA) + 3 mg glimepiride | FBG, HbA1c, HOMA-IR ↓ vs drug alone; irisin, HDL, SIRT1 ↑ |
| 3-mo high-dose EPA+DHA, obesity women n=12 + lean n=12 | 4.8 g/d (3.2 EPA + 1.6 DHA) | Insulin, HOMA-IR, TG/HDL, TNF-α ↓; HOMA-IR below 2.5; immune transcripts suppressed 1 mo post-stop |
| 12-wk SPM-mechanism RCT, T2D n=60 | 4 g fish oil (1.8 EPA + 1.2 DHA) | RvE1 ×3.1, PD1 ×2.7, TNF-α -38%, CRP -45% |
| 12-wk calanus-oil RCT, obese IR n=266 | 2-4 g/d ± lifestyle | HOMA ↓ with 2 g + lifestyle (p=0.021); -0.76 with 4 g in mild IR |
| 3-wk EPA-rich burns RCT n=24 | 1.5 g EPA + 0.6 g DHA/d | HOMA-IR, FBS, insulin, CRP, ESR ↓ vs control |
| VITAL nested case-control (5.3 y) | 1 g/d n-3 | No modification of IRS→CVD/T2D at this low dose; suggests dose + baseline IR matter; CHD benefit signal in high-IR subgroups |

Caveat: 1 g/d in already well-nourished low-IR populations shows neutral T2D incidence; benefit concentrates in HOMA-IR >2.5, high TG, high inflammation, low baseline omega-3 index. This matches FIT principle: correct deficiency/noise, don't mega-dose signal.

## 4. Beans + Fatty Fish Together: Why the Pair Is More Than the Sum

1. **Gut + membrane:** beans ferment in colon (SCFA/GLP-1/bile-acid axis); EPA/DHA resolve in tissue (SPM/NF-κB axis). One lowers glucose influx, the other restores glucose uptake.
2. **Liver double-hit:** bean fiber/SCFA + fish TG-lowering both de-fat liver → DAG/PKCε relief → fasting insulin falls. Iranian legume trial ALT -46% + fish MASLD trials point same way.
3. **Protein complement without saturated-fat load:** beans (lysine-rich, methionine-low) + fish (methionine/lysine/leucine-complete, B12, D, selenium) cover amino acids and firmware at low trophic cost. Replaces red/processed meat pattern that raises T2D risk via heme iron, saturated fat, AGEs, TMAO, BCAAs.
4. **Microbiome + inflammation loop:** high fiber feeds SCFA producers; EPA/DHA + SPMs prevent endotoxin (LPS)-driven TLR4 resistance from leaky gut. Fermented pairings (beans + kefir/yogurt/kimchi, fish + fermented sides) amplify synbiotic effect — see `DIET.md`.
5. **Practical FIT plate:** beans provide bulk/energy-density dilution; small fish provides flavor/condiment satiety at 5% calories. Adherence beats either alone.

No RCT has tested beans + sardines vs control for clamp sensitivity yet — this is a gap. Inference rests on convergent RCTs of each arm + dietary-pattern cohorts (Mediterranean, Blue Zones, Okinawan soy+fish) where legumes + fish co-occur with low HOMA-IR and exceptional longevity.

## 5. Practical FIT-IS Protocol

### How much is enough?

- **Beans:** 1 cup cooked (~170-200 g) daily, or ≥4 cups/week minimum. Mix: lentils, black/kidney/navy beans, chickpeas, split peas, soy/natto/tempeh. Rinse canned, cook from dry with soaking to reduce phytate/lectin noise while preserving cell-wall matrix. Cool some portions (bean salads) for extra RS3.
- **Fatty low-trophic fish:** 2-3 servings/week, 100-140 g each, totaling ~1.5-2.5 g EPA+DHA/week from food. Prioritize sardines, anchovies, herring, mackerel, wild salmon. Avoid large predators (tuna steak, swordfish, shark) as staples — Class C in FIT due to mercury/PCB bioaccumulation.
- **If fish unavailable:** consider 1-2 g/d combined EPA+DHA from tested triglyceride/phospholipid oil, with clinician input, especially if TG high or omega-3 index <8%. Food first.

### Pairing ideas (see also `DIET.md` Pairing Ideas)

- Lentil-mushroom stew + sardines on whole-grain toast + sauerkraut
- Black-bean chili + small aged cheese + kefir side
- Chickpea + tuna/sardine + olive + kimchi bowl over brown rice/barley
- Natto/tempeh + brown rice + miso soup + grilled mackerel
- Navy-bean + kale + garlic soup + herring + yogurt
- Oats + wheat germ + kefir breakfast, beans + fish at lunch/dinner (spread fiber across day for second-meal effect)

### What to keep at zero

Sugary drinks, white flour, sweets, ultra-processed snacks, refined-seed-oil-fried foods — they erase bean/fish gains within hours via Speed/Matrix Toxicity. See `IR.md` §6.

### Track sensitivity, not just weight

Fasting glucose + insulin → HOMA-IR every 8-12 weeks, HbA1c, TG/HDL, waist, ALT. Goal trajectory: HOMA-IR → <2.0 → <1.5 → ~1.0, then shorten fasting interval per `IR.md` formula. If HOMA-IR ≥3 on medication, do not fast extended without medical supervision.

## 6. Evidence Caveats and Open Questions

- Most bean/fish RCTs are weeks-to-months, n=20-260, with HOMA-IR/fasting insulin — not clamp — as outcome. Clamp data for centenarian longevity is observational. Causality for lifespan extension in humans is not proven; inference is mechanistic + epidemiological.
- Heterogeneity is high: baseline HOMA-IR, BMI, microbiome, omega-3 index, bean type/dose, fish species, background diet, and drug use all modify effect. Non-responders exist.
- Fiber + high-protein interactions can be complex: one 12-week RCT of potato-fiber/pectin on top of 25% protein diet found no peripheral-sensitivity gain (even slight fall) vs placebo — context matters; whole beans ≠ isolated fiber + isolated protein.
- High-dose fish oil can raise LDL-C in some, prolong bleeding time, and oxidize if poorly stored. Choose whole fish, cold storage, low-heat cooking; discuss dose with clinician if on anticoagulants.
- Fermented-fish data on spermidine (see `DIET.md`) should not be confused with fresh fatty-fish EPA/DHA data here — different molecules, complementary benefits.
- No longevity RCT of beans+fish vs control will ever be feasible for lifespan; we rely on biomarker bridging (HOMA-IR → mortality cohorts → centenarian clamp).

Treat this as well-motivated FIT pattern, not prescription.
