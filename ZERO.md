# ZERO: The Zero-Calorie Interval Between Meals

> **FIT Protocol for Pancreatic Rest, Low Insulin, Autophagy, and Mitochondrial Renewal — No Supplements Required**

> **Medical disclaimer:** This document is educational information only, not medical advice. If you have diabetes, prediabetes, insulin resistance, obesity, or take glucose-lowering medication (insulin, sulfonylureas, meglitinides, SGLT2-inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists), blood pressure medication, or have a history of eating disorders, pregnancy/breastfeeding, gout, kidney or liver disease — talk to your physician before changing meal frequency, skipping meals, or extending fasts. Fasting can cause hypoglycemia, hypotension, dizziness, electrolyte shifts, and drug interactions. Monitor glucose, do not drive hypoglycemic, and seek care for symptoms.

## 1. The ZERO Rule

**Between meals: ZERO calories.**

- No snacks. No juice. No milk in coffee. No "small bites." No sweetened drinks. No protein bars.
- Water, plain mineral water, unsweetened tea, black coffee if tolerated — only.
- Eat complete FIT meals, then close the channel until the next meal.
- **Maybe skip a meal now and then.** If you are not hungry in the morning, skip breakfast. If you had a late, full lunch, skip dinner. Do not eat by the clock. Eat by true hunger after a full FIT refeed.

In FIT terms (see `README.md`):

- A calorie is a carrier wave. What matters is signal vs. noise.
- **Frequent eating = constant noise.** Every caloric hit raises glucose, raises insulin, suppresses lipolysis, suppresses autophagy, and keeps the pancreas on shift with no rest.
- **ZERO between meals = silence on the channel.** Glucose falls. Insulin falls. The receiver can finally decode, repair, and renew.

This does not require calorie counting, supplements, powders, or longevity pills. It requires *not doing* something: not eating between meals.

Related: `IR.md` (scale the interval to HOMA-IR), `DIET.md` (what to eat when you eat), `AUTOPHAGY.md` (fasting + exercise + hormesis triggers).

## 2. Give the Pancreas Rest So It Can Regenerate

The pancreas, especially insulin-producing beta-cells, is among the most nutrient-sensitive tissues in the body.

Chronic grazing on refined foods forces chronic insulin secretion:

1. **Speed Toxicity:** refined sugar/flour floods blood in minutes → massive insulin demand.
2. **Matrix Toxicity:** no fiber to slow absorption → no brake.
3. **Signal Toxicity:** incomplete data without co-factors → hunger returns fast → you eat again.

Result: hyperinsulinemia all day, beta-cell exhaustion, insulin resistance, fat locked in storage, higher HbA1c, weight gain.

ZERO breaks that loop:

- A clean inter-meal interval lets glucose return to baseline, then lets insulin fall *after* glucose normalizes. Insulin stays high much longer than glucose in insulin-resistant people — that is why `IR.md` uses `HOMA-IR × 4h × 2` as a heuristic.
- Repeated fast-refeed cycles in mice switch on developmental programs: Sox17 → Pdx-1 → Ngn3-driven generation of new insulin-producing beta-cells, restoring insulin secretion and glucose homeostasis in both type 1 and type 2 diabetes models. Fasting conditions reduce IGF-1 / PKA / mTOR signaling, the key priming step. In human pancreatic islets ex vivo, fasting conditions induce Sox2/Ngn3 expression and insulin production (Cheng et al., *Cell* 2017).
- In humans, fewer meals and time-restricted eating improve glycemic control without necessarily cutting total calories: 1 meal/d vs. 3 meals/d at isocaloric intake maintained weight with lower fasting glucose (Stote et al., *Am J Clin Nutr* 2007); 16:8 or 14:10 done 3 days/week for 3 months reduced weight, fasting glucose, HbA1c and lipids in obese type 2 diabetes (Sukriang et al., *J Diabetes Investig* 2024); 8-hour time-restricted eating (12:00–20:00) for 6 months produced ~3.6% weight loss vs. control and lowered HbA1c similarly to 25% calorie restriction, with better adherence (Varady et al., *JAMA Network Open* 2023, NIH-funded RCT); a 5:2 intermittent fasting meal-replacement pattern lowered HbA1c by 1.9% in newly diagnosed overweight/obese type 2 diabetes, comparable to metformin and empagliflozin (EARLY trial, Guo et al., *JAMA Network Open* 2024).

Implications:

- **Diabetes:** less insulin demand per day, flatter curves, lower HOMA-IR over time, chance to graduate to shorter intervals (see `IR.md` §5 virtuous cycle).
- **Obesity:** when insulin is low for hours, hormone-sensitive lipase is disinhibited and stored triglyceride can be mobilized and oxidized. Same calories, less storage lock.
- **Longevity:** stable glucose + lower insulin/IGF-1 signaling is one of the most conserved longevity pathways from yeast to monkeys to CALERIE humans. See §5.

Caveat: beta-cell regeneration is proven in mice and human islets *ex vivo*, not yet proven as durable regeneration in living humans. Treat fasting as pancreatic *rest* with regenerative potential, not as a guaranteed cure. Larger FDA trials were explicitly called for by Longo and colleagues.

## 3. When Insulin Is Low, Autophagy Happens

Autophagy is the cell's organized recycling: sequester misfolded proteins, damaged organelles, lipid droplets, and intracellular pathogens into autophagosomes, deliver to lysosomes, degrade, reuse.

It is inhibited by nutrients and activated by their absence:

- **Fed:** insulin / IGF-1 → PI3K → Akt → mTORC1 ON → ULK1 complex phosphorylated and suppressed → autophagy OFF. Secretory granules, including insulin granules, are spared; storage mode dominates.
- **Fasted:** glucose low → ATP/AMP falls → AMPK ON → TSC2 / Raptor phosphorylation → mTORC1 OFF → ULK1 de-repressed and activated by AMPK → autophagosome formation ON. SIRT1 and TFEB add transcriptional drive (Atg genes, lysosomal biogenesis).

Evidence:

- The core AMPK-mTOR-ULK1 switch was defined by Egan et al., *Science* 2011 and Kim et al., *Mol Cell* 2011: under glucose starvation AMPK promotes autophagy by directly activating ULK1 while inhibiting mTORC1.
- In pancreatic beta-cells specifically, mTORC1 is inhibited by fasting and rapidly re-stimulated by refeeding/leucine/glucose. Inhibiting mTORC1 mimics fasting: autophagy rises, insulin secretion is held low to prevent hypoglycemia; moderate autophagy inhibition rescues secretion. This is physiological fasting-feeding coupling, not pathology (Goginashvili et al., *Diabetes* 2015; reviewed in Front Endocrinol 2025).
- A 2025 narrative review of preclinical and clinical intermittent-fasting studies concludes fasting robustly raises AMPK phosphorylation, inhibits mTOR, and raises LC3-II, Beclin-1 and ATG proteins in metabolically active tissues, improving insulin sensitivity, hepatic lipid, and clearance of toxic aggregates (Vergara Nieto et al., *Curr Nutr Rep* 2025).
- Classic FIT complement: spermidine-rich + fiber-fed microbiome provides baseline autophagy tone via EP300 inhibition even when you eat (Madeo et al., *Science* 2018; see `DIET.md` and `AUTOPHAGY.md` §1). ZERO adds the *fasting* pulse. Food sets the tone, absence sets the peak.

Practical translation: you do not need an autophagy supplement. The most potent, free mTOR inhibitor is *no calories for hours*. Every zero-calorie stretch between meals is a small cleanup shift. Skipping a meal occasionally extends that shift to 16–24h+, where systemic autophagy ramps markedly.

## 4. Low Insulin Promotes Mitochondrial Biogenesis

Mitochondria age. They leak, mutate, produce excess ROS, and get cleared by mitophagy (selective autophagy of mitochondria). Health is not just clearance — it is clearance *plus* replacement: mitochondrial biogenesis, building new, efficient mitochondria.

Low-insulin / low-energy states activate the builders:

- **AMPK → PGC-1α:** the master co-activator of mitochondrial biogenesis. AMPK phosphorylates PGC-1α (Thr-177/Ser-538) and drives its transcription. SIRT1 deacetylates and further activates PGC-1α. PGC-1α then co-activates NRF-1/2, ERRs, PPARs → TFAM → nuclear + mitochondrial DNA transcription → new mitochondria.
- **Fasting → hepatic PGC-1α:** via glucagon-cAMP-CREB and p38 MAPK (Fernandez-Marcos & Auwerx, *Am J Clin Nutr* 2011 review).
- **Calorie restriction → mitochondrial biogenesis:** via eNOS/NO signaling in mammals (Nisoli et al., *Science* 2005); skeletal-muscle PGC-1α is required for the full CR-induced rise in mitochondrial gene expression and density in oxidative fibers (Wenz et al., *PNAS* 2012). In healthy humans, 6 months of calorie restriction increased muscle mitochondrial biogenesis markers (Civitarese et al., *PLoS Med* 2007).
- **Urolithin A / spermidine / exercise** converge on the same renewal cycle from the food and movement side: stimulate mitophagy (PINK1/Parkin, BNIP3L) while SIRT1/PGC-1α drives biogenesis (see `DIET.md` §Health Benefits 1 and `AUTOPHAGY.md` §2).

Implications for anti-aging and longevity:

- More, better mitochondria = higher fat oxidation, better glucose disposal, lower ROS per ATP, preserved muscle and brain function — the exact capacities that decline with age and predict survival.
- You cannot supplement your way to this. Mitochondrial turnover requires the *signal of absence*: low insulin, high AMPK, active sirtuins. That signal is free every time you leave hours with ZERO calories and move a little.

## 5. You Need No Supplement for a Long, Happy Life

FIT is explicit: vitality comes from high signal (diverse whole plants + fermented foods + 5% B12 layer) and low noise (zero refined fragments), delivered with *silent intervals*. No pill reproduces that.

Why food + ZERO beats bottles:

1. **Supplements often do not raise tissue levels.** High-dose spermidine supplementation did not increase blood spermidine in healthy humans (Senekowitsch et al., *Nutrients* 2023), while daily natto (~66 g/d for 2 months) raised whole-blood spermine ~1.4× and modulated inflammation (Soda et al., *J Nutr Sci Vitaminol* 2009; *Med Sci* 2021; reviewed in Madeo et al., *Science* 2018).
2. **Fiber + ferments are synbiotic, not single-molecule.** Oats + kefir, beans + aged cheese, broccoli + kimchi feed *Bacteroides*, *Fusobacterium* and LAB that make spermidine/putrescine around the clock in the colon, while increasing microbiome diversity and lowering inflammatory markers (Wastyk et al., *Cell* 2021; Tofalo et al., *Front Nutr* 2019; see `DIET.md` tables).
3. **Longevity in humans tracks patterns, not pills.** Higher dietary spermidine intake linked to lower mortality in Bruneck/SAPHIR cohorts (Kiechl et al., *Am J Clin Nutr* 2018, observational); Blue Zones average ~5% animal intake with daily legumes, whole grains, and ferments (see `README.md` §3); CALERIE showed 2-year ~15–25% calorie restriction in non-obese humans slows biological aging markers without supplements; time-restricted eating trials show metabolic benefit from *timing* alone (Varady 2023; Sukkriang 2024; EARLY 2024).
4. **Hormesis cannot be swallowed.** Exercise AMPK, sauna/cold HSPs, bitter polyphenols, and ZERO itself are mild stresses that trigger repair. A capsule that bypasses effort bypasses adaptation (see `AUTOPHAGY.md` §2–3).

Eat FIT when you eat. Drink water between. Move, sleep, connect. Repeat for decades. That is the whole stack.

## 6. Practical ZERO Protocol

### What counts as ZERO

- Allowed: water, sparkling mineral water, plain tea, black coffee, electrolytes if advised by clinician.
- Not allowed: juice, soda, milk, sugar, honey, broth with calories, gum with sugar, "just a bite," tasting while cooking, liquid calories of any kind.
- Medications as prescribed. If a medication must be taken with food, follow your doctor's instruction — that meal becomes your meal.

### How to start

1. **First, fix what you eat (1–2 weeks).** Full FIT meals to satiety: legumes + whole grains + vegetables + nuts/seeds + fermented daily + 5% B12 anchor. Eliminate Class D refined noise. No stable meals → no stable fasts.
2. **Then fix when you eat.** 3 FIT meals, ZERO between, 12h overnight (e.g. 19:00 → 07:00). No snacking. This alone is HOMA-IR ~1.0–1.5 territory (see `IR.md`).
3. **Then skip occasionally.** Once or twice a week, skip the meal you least miss — often breakfast or a late dinner — to create a 16–24h ZERO stretch. Walk after refeeds, sleep 7.5–9h.
4. **Scale only if needed.** If HOMA-IR ≥2–5, use `IR.md` §3 table to extend systematically (16h → 20h → 24h → 32–40h) with clinician approval. Do not stack extended fasts back-to-back unsupervised.
5. **Break fasts with FIT, not noise.** Vegetables/legumes/fermented + protein/fat first, then whole grains. Never break a long ZERO with sugar/flour.

### Sample day (maintenance)

- 07:00–09:00: light, water, green tea/black coffee, walk. Delay breakfast if not hungry.
- 12:00: FIT lunch to satiety. ZERO after.
- 18:30: FIT dinner (crucifers, legumes, whole grain, fermented, small B12 portion). ZERO after.
- 21:30: dim lights, cool room, consistent sleep.

Skipped-meal variant: lunch → ZERO → next-day brunch (~20–24h). Or dinner → ZERO through next day → dinner (~24h). Pick the rhythm you can repeat without misery.

Stop and consult for: hypo symptoms, dizziness, palpitations, vomiting, chest pain, gout flare, fainting, or inability to eat adequately in refeeds. Extended fasting is contraindicated in pregnancy/breastfeeding, history of eating disorder, advanced kidney/liver disease, and frail elderly without supervision.

## 7. Evidence Caveat

- Pancreatic beta-cell regeneration: strong in mice + human islets ex vivo (Cheng 2017), not yet established as durable regeneration in living humans. Human diabetes reversal trials with fasting show improved glycemia/weight but small samples, short duration, variable protocols.
- Autophagy in humans cannot yet be measured directly in most tissues; LC3-II/Beclin-1/ATG markers and AMPK/mTOR readouts are proxies, mostly from blood, muscle biopsies, or animal models.
- Mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α is necessary for full CR response in mouse oxidative muscle but not for all whole-body metabolic benefits; human data (Civitarese 2007) are supportive but from small CALERIE-type cohorts.
- Meal-skipping trials are isocaloric or small (Stote n=15 crossover; Sukkriang 3d/week; Varady n=75, 6 months). Breakfast-skipping may worsen post-lunch glucose/inflammation vs. dinner-skipping in acute crossover (Jakubowicz /ada; Nas et al., *Am J Clin Nutr* 2017: breakfast-skipping raised lunch glucose +54% and inflammatory potential). Prefer skipping dinner/late eating over skipping breakfast if glucose control is the goal, unless personal adherence clearly favors mornings — track and adjust.
- The `HOMA-IR × 4h × 2` rule in `IR.md` is a pragmatic FIT heuristic, not a validated guideline.

None of this changes the core advice: ZERO calories between meals is safe for most healthy adults, free, and directionally supported across mechanisms and trials. Eat well, space well, skip sometimes, re-test HOMA-IR/HbA1c/waist every 8–12 weeks, and let the trend — not a supplement label — tell you it works.
